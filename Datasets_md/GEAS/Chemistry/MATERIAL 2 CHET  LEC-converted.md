## Second Law of Thermodynamics

The First Law identifies both heat and work as methods of energy transfer which can bring about a change in the internal energy of a system but it fails to describe the final state of the system. The second law of thermodynamics focuses on the direction of energy flow. It states that it is not possible for heat to flow from colder body to hotter body without any work having been done to accomplish this flow

The Second Law of Thermodynamics also states that the entropy (S) (the measure of the degree of randomness of a system) of the universe is increasing .

For a spontaneous process:

ΔS univ = ΔS sys + ΔS surr &gt; 0 (ΔS univ = +)

For an equilibrium process:

ΔS univ = ΔS sys + ΔS surr = 0 (ΔS univ = 0)

The total entropy change is defined as the sum of the change in entropy of the system and the change in entropy of the surroundings. So, to determine whether or not the process is spontaneous, the entropy of the system, surroundings and universe must be calculated.
