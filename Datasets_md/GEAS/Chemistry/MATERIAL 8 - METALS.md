## Metallic Bonding

The conduction of heat and electricity that characterize metals is due to their distinctive kind of bonding. The solid metals behave as if they are composed of positive ions in a sea  of  electrons.  In  metallic  crystal,  positively  charged  ions  occupy  position  in  the crystal structure and the loosely bound electrons move freely throughout the crystalline structure.  These  mobile  electron  clouds  (also  called  'sea  of  electron)  around  the positive kernel (the nucleus plus the filled energy levels) bind the atoms together. The non-directional  attraction  between  positive  ions  and  the  electron  cloud  constitute  the metallic  bond .  The  close-packed  crystal  structure  of  metals  points  out  that  metallic bond is strong and because metal ions in an electron cloud are uniformly proportional, their positions can be altered yet crystal structure remains.

## CORROSION PREVENTION OF METALS

- Environmental Modification
- Corrosion is caused by chemical interactions between metal and gasses in the surrounding environment. By removing the metal from, or changing, the type of environment, metal deterioration can be immediately reduced.
- This may be as simple as limiting contact with rain or seawater by storing metal materials indoors or could be in the form of direct manipulation of the environmental affecting the metal
- Methods  to  reduce  the  sulfur,  chloride,  or  oxygen  content  in  the  surrounding environment can limit the speed of metal corrosion. For example, feed water for water boilers can be treated with softeners or other chemical media to adjust the hardness, alkalinity or oxygen content in order to reduce corrosion on the interior of the unit.
- Monitoring  of  surface  conditions  is  also  critical  in  protecting  against  metal deterioration from corrosion. Cracks, crevices or asperous surfaces, whether a result of operational requirements, wear and tear, or manufacturing flaws, all can result in greater rates of corrosion

## Semiconductor

F. Bloch proposed a quantum mechanical theory, called band theory , to account for the bonding of the atoms in metallic crystals. In this theory, all electrons in an atom in completely filled energy levels are considered to be essentially restricted or bound to the  atoms  with  which  they  are  associated.  The  valence  electrons  in  unfilled  energy levels, on the other hand, are considered to be free, and move in a potential field which extends  over  all  the  atoms  present  in  the  crystal.  The  atomic  orbital  of  these  free electrons (valence electrons) in one atom can overlap those of the electrons in other atom to yield delocalized molecular orbital which produces bonding among all the atoms present.  The  interaction  of  molecular  orbitals  form  what  is  called  a band .  The  band formed  by  the  valence  electrons  in  the  atoms  constitutes  the valence  band .  For example, in the Na-Na molecule- 1s 2  2s 2 2p 6   3s 1   - the 3s band make up the valence band. On the other hand, the band formed from the empty orbital by the same energy is called  the conduction  band .  For  Na-Na  molecule,  the  3p  orbitals  make  up  the conduction band. The  figure below illustrates the difference between  insulator, conductor and semiconductor in terms of their band energy.

In  a  conductor  like  metals,  the  valence  band  is  close  to  the  conduction  band hence, there is negligible amount of energy needed to transfer valence electrons to the conduction  band.  In  an  insulator  like  rubber,  there  is  a  great  gap  between  the conduction  band  and  the  valence  band;  hence,  a  considerable  amount  of  energy  to excite electron into the conduction band.

In  semiconductors,  the  energy  gap  between  the  conduction  band  and  the valence band is much smaller than that for insulators. If the energy needed to excite electrons  from  the  valence  band  to  the  conduction  band  is  provided,  the  solid  can become a conductor. The conductivity can be enhanced by increasing the temperature or  by  adding  small  amounts  of  certain  other  elements.  However,  an  increased  in temperature will decrease the conductivity of metals since the increased vibration will disrupt the flow of electrons.

## Doping of Semiconductor

A  pure  crystalline  substance  that  functions  as  a  semiconductor  is  called  an intrinsic  semiconductor .  When  an  impurity  atom  was  added  to  it  to  enhance  its conductivity it is now referred to as extrinsic semiconductor . The current that flow in an intrinsic semiconductor consists of electron current and hole current. That is when a voltage is applied the electrons which have been freed from their lattice positions into the conduction band can move through the crystal.

Figure 6.3: Sea of electron

<!-- image -->

Some electrons hop between lattice positions to fill the vacancies left by the free electrons. This process is called hole conduction because it is as if the holes were the ones  migrating  across  the  crystal  in  the  direction  opposite  to  the  free  electron movement.

The electrical properties of semiconductors such as silicon and germanium can be enhanced by adding small amounts of foreign atoms in their regular crystal lattice. This process is called doping. In this process n-type and p-type semiconductors may be produced.

In n-type  semiconductor ,  pentavalent  (5  valence  electrons)  impurities  are added. Notice that the impurity atoms are in group V of the periodic table while silicon is in  group  IV.  When  pentavalent  elements  are  incorporated  to  the  silicon  crystals  they tend to contribute an extra electron to the conduction band. If voltage is applied across the  solid,  the  extra  electrons  can  be  removed  from  the  donor  impurity  and  such  can move  through  the  structure  and  function  as  conduction  electron.  The  name  (n-type) comes from the fact that there is an added electron, which is negative charge.

A p-type semiconductor on the other hand uses trivalent (3 valence electrons) impurity atoms such as boron, aluminum and gallium which will contribute a hole to the valence band. If boron atom with three valence electrons (1s 2  2s 2  2p 1 ) will replace one silicon  atoms,  there  is  an  electron  vacancy  or  positive  hole.  If  an  electron  a  nearby silicon  atom  is  excited  to  the  hole,  the  vacancy  in  silicon  atom  can  be  filled  by  an electron from another neighboring silicon atom and so on. The electrons then move in one direction through the structure while the positive holes in the opposite direction and the solid becomes a conductor.

The  presence  of  impurity  atoms  reduces  the  energy  needed  to  excite  the  valence electrons to the conduction band. The conductivity of semiconductor is also increased by a factor of 100,000 or so.

Doped  semiconductors  are  at  the  heart  of  the  modern  electronics  industry. Thousands of diodes, transistors, and other circuit element can fit into a small integrated circuit  or  silicon  chip.  And  smaller  and  smaller  chips  are  doing  jobs  faster.  An  entire computer,  called  microprocessor  can  be  built  on  the  tiny  chip  a  millimeter  or  so  in diameter.

A process is applied to sulfide ores of copper, lead and zinc where the crushed ore is  mixed with water and ground into soap- like slurry. The mixture will then be transferred to a floatation tank. Then, suitable oil and detergent will be added. The oil particles adhere to the mineral particles while water particles adhere to the gangue particles. Agitation of the mixture with air (air  is blown through the mixture)  causes  the  oil-mineral  particle  mixture  to  float  on  top  of  the  mixture, while the sand and dirt settle to the bottom of the tank. The froth will then be skimmed off while the gangue will be removed from the bottom of the tank

## 3.  Magnetic Separation

Used to separate magnetic, Fe3O4, from the gangue. In this process, electromagnets  are  used  to  attract  the  ferromagnetic  Fe3O4.  The  crushed  ore containing  magnetite  is  passed  through  a  powerful  magnet  and  the  Fe3O4 particles are attracted to the poles of the magnet.

4.  Liquation

In this process, the native ores of bismuth and copper is heated just above the melting point of the metals.

## 5.  Amalgation

Used to synthesize silver and gold. The crushed native ores of silver and gold are treated with mercury. The mercury will then be removed by distillation.

## 6.  Chemical Separation

Based on the chemical properties of the mineral. Ex. Bayer Method

Bauxite (Al2O3), an aluminum ore, may be concentrated by mixing it with concentrated  NaOH  solution.  Since  Al2O3  is  amphoteric,  it  dissolves  in NaOH while the impurities (ferric oxide and Silicates) are not.

The impurities will be filtered and the solution will be neutralized with acid which causes the formation of precipitates.

## 7.  Reduction

The process employed to reduce metal compounds to free metal.

## 7.1 Smelting

A process that employs chemical reduction at high temperature (until the ore molten). This process is sometimes called Pyrometallurgy. In this process the ore is heated with a reductant to obtain the metal. This process is used in steel industries in the production of iron in the blast furnace.

<!-- formula-not-decoded -->

In this method the actual reductant is CO, which reduces Fe3O4 to Fe and CO2. The CO2 is then reduced back to CO by the reaction with excess carbon.

Figure : Blast Furnace

<!-- image -->

This  reaction  occurs  in  a  huge  blast  furnace.  The  charge  (mixture  of  iron  ore, limestone  and  coke)  is  loaded  on  top  of  the  furnace.  The  coke  in  this  process serves as the reducing agent, which reduces iron oxide, Fe2O3 , to its free state, while limestone react with the high melting impurities to form a slag, which has a lower meting point. The slag will then be drained at the bottom of the tank. Molten iron run out the lowermost part of the furnace, leaving the slag behind. The iron was collected in pools called pigs, which is the origin of the name pig iron.

## 7.2 Metal Displacement

Use metals which is more reactive than the desired product

## 7.3 Hydrogen reduction

Reduction of metal oxide such as GeO2, WO3 and MoO3 to its free state by reaction with hydrogen gas.

## 7.4 Hydrometallurgy

In this process, chemical or electrochemical reduction of aqueous solution of a salt of the metal is used. It is applied in extraction of less electropositive or less reactive metals like gold and silver.

## 8.  Leaching

This process mixes aqueous solution with the compound containing the metal of  interest.  Oxidation potential,  temperature  and  pH  of  the  mixture  are  then manipulated  to  optimize  the  dissolution  (leaching)  of  the  desired  metal component  into  the  aqueous  phase.  Heap  leaching  or  acid  leaching  of uranium is one of the popular example of this process.

## 9.  Solution concentration and purification

Normally, the leach liquor undergo concentration of the important metals ions and removal of the undesirable metal ions.

- 1.1  Precipitation  -  selective  removal  of  targeted  metal  or  removal  of  major impurity.
- 1.2  Froth  flotation  -  separation  of  hydrophobic  materials  from  hydrophilic materials.

## 2.  Metal recovery

The raw materials is often produced in this step but sometimes further refining is  required  if  ultra-high  purity  metals  are  to  be  manufactured.  Electrolysis, gaseous  reduction  and  further  precipitation  are  the  primary  steps  in  metal recovery. Copper for example is conveniently obtained by electrolysis.
