## MATERIAL 9 - POLYMERS

## Introduction to Polymers

Polymer is a  large  molecule  composed  of  repeating  small  molecule  units.  The term 'polymer' was derived from the Greek words poly and meros , which means many and parts, respectively. We often associate polymers with the term plastics. In reality, these  plastics  are  just  some  examples  of  synthetic  polymers  commonly  encountered and used daily such as grocery bags and water bottles.

The small molecule precursor or building block of polymers is called a monomer. For example, polyethylene which is commonly used in pipes, cable coverings, and toys is  derived  from  ethene  (Fig.  7.1a).  Often,  the  chemical  structure  of  polymers  is represented  by  the  monomer  enclosed  in  a  parenthesis  (Fig  7.1b).  The  number  of repeating monomer units is represented by the subscript n.

## Some Applications of Polymers

1.  Natural polymers such as proteins, nucleic acids, and polysaccharides serve as the basic functional unit of plant and animal life.
2.  Natural polymers present in meat, vegetables, and rice serve as food.
3.  Polymers  can  be  used  as  construction  materials  specifically  as  concrete,  and insulation.
4.  Polymers can be crafted into light materials for transportation such as in aircrafts, automobiles, ships, and trucks.
5.  Polymers  can  be  used  as  packaging  materials  such  as  food  and  water containers.

## Thermoplastics vs. Thermosets Polymer

Generally, polymers can be classified into two major classifications: thermoplastics and thermosets.

Thermoplastics are  polymers  that  can  be  melted  and  reshaped  by applying heat and pressure. The ability to melt at reasonable temperatures  becomes  a  major  advantage.  The  fact  that  the  material softens or melts when heated allows shaping it into the desired form. In a thermoplastic polymer, chains interact only through intermolecular force.

Generally, most linear polymers are thermoplastics such as polyethylene, polypropylene, and polystyrene.

Thermosets are polymers that readily  undergo degradation  upon application  of  heat  and  pressure  before  they  melt  and  reshaped. Thermosetting polymers maintain their shape and strength when heated. The name 'thermosetting' comes from the fact that these polymers must be heated to 'set' or 'lock  in'  their  structures.  But  once  this  has  been done, the material offer increased strength and do not lose their shape upon further heating.

The  presence  of  crosslinks  or  covalent  bonds  that  attached  one polymer  main  chain  to  another  polymer  main  chain  is  a  common characteristic of thermosetting polymers (e.g. vulcanized rubber). ## Isotactic, Syndiotactic, and Atactic Polymers

When  the  monomer  is  less  symmetrical  than  ethylene,  there  is  additional variability that might result from the polymerization process.

Isotactic - the side chain is arranged on one side of the backbone chain.

<!-- image -->

Syndiotactic - polymers where the side chain is arranged alternatively on both sides of the backbone chain.

<!-- image -->

Atactic -    polymers  where  the  side  chain  is  arranged  randomly  along  the backbone chain .

<!-- image -->

The orientation of the side chain (whether is arranged on one side or not) affects the properties of the polymers. One example is polypropylene (PP); isotactic, syndiotactic and atactic PPhave different characteristics. Isotactic polypropylene is crystalline  and  its  Tm  (melting  temperature)  is  around  160-170°C  and  have  high strength  and  rigidity  and  can  even  be  made  into  fibres.  Syndiotactic  PP  is  semi crystalline and its Tm is around 120-130°C and atactic is amorphous, rubbery and flexible.

## Copolymers

Nylon is an example of copolymer made from more than one type of monomer.

Alternating polymers - monomers are arranged in a regular, alternating series. Block copolymers - have regions in the material where a single monomer unit is  repeated,  interspersed  with  other  regions  where  a  different  monomer  is  the repeating unit.

Random copolymers - monomers are randomly combined.

Graft  copolymers -  involve  side  chains  of  one  polymer  attached  to  a backbone of a different polymer.

<!-- image -->

The  specific  composition  of  copolymer  can  be  tailored  to  a  particular application.  For  example,  Spandex  is  an  example  of  block  copolymer.  In  this material,  some  regions  of  the  polymer  are  relatively  stiff,  whereas  others  are flexible. The resulting material is both strong and flexible, leading to widespread application in the field of sport gear and fashion.

## Properties of Polymers

The properties of the polymers are usually governed by its structure. One such important property of polymers that is being analysed is the glass transition temperature. The glass transition temperature (Tg) is the temperature range where a polymer chain gains segmental motion. The flexibility of the polymer chains depends on (1)  the  presence  of  stiffening  and  flexibilizing  groups  incorporated  in  the  polymer backbone and (2) the presence of functional groups attached to the polymer backbone. These functional groups attached to the polymer backbone is called pendant groups.

For example, polyethylene terephthalate (PET) has a higher glass transition temperature  than  polyethylene  adipate.  The  terephthalic  acid  moiety  in  polyethylene terephthalate (PET) is considerably an inflexible unit. Furthermore, the terephthalic acid moiety stiffens the polymer backbone and restricts polymer chain mobility. On the other hand,  the  adipic  acid  moiety  in  polyethylene  adipate  is  flexible;  thus,  making  the polymer backbone chains more freely mobile. The effect of pendant groups in the glass transition temperature is very evident by comparing the glass transition temperatures of low  density  polyethylene  (LDPE)  and  polyvinyl  alcohol  (PVA).  PVA  has  a  relatively higher glass transition temperature than LDPE attributed to the presence of the hydroxyl group  (-OH)  which  can  form  strong  hydrogen  bonds  with  other  polymer  chains. Whereas in LDPE, each polymer chains can only interact with each other via the much weaker  London  dispersion  forces.  The  glass  transition  temperature  of  polymers  also increase due to polymer backbone symmetry and the presence of crosslinks. Adding additives  such  as  plasticizers,  on  the  other  hand,  decreases  the  glass  transition temperatures of polymers.

Another  important  property  of  polymers  is  its melting  temperature .  Melting temperature (Tm) is the temperature range in which the whole polymer chains become mobile.  Synonymous  to  Tg,  the  melting  temperature  is  higher  for  polymers  having stiffening  groups  in  the  backbone  and  pendant  groups  that  may  form  stronger intermolecular forces of attraction.

## Naturally-occurring polymers

Naturally-occurring  polymers  are  ubiquitous  in  nature.  These  natural  polymers perform certain tasks in every living organisms such as animals and plants that are vital for their existence such as structural support, and some biological functions.

Glucose,  the  most  abundant  monosaccharide,  is  the  primary  chemical  energy storage of plants via photosynthesis. Cellulose and starch are two naturally-occurring polymers  derived  from  glucose  that  only  differs  in  the  configuration  of  the  linkage between carbon 1 and carbon 4. Cellulose, a linear polymer of glucose, is the major structural  component  of  plant  cell  walls.  It  is  the  most  abundant  organic  polymer material on earth. Cellulose is the major constituent of cotton, kapok, flax, hemp, jute, ramie, and wood. As we know it, cellulosic fibers such as cotton is very significant and widely  used  in  the  textile  industry  for  cloths,  cartons,  blankets,  and  sheets.  Paper  is another important cellulosic product that has been used around 2 nd  century AD. Paper has been widely used up until now as a means of communication such as newspapers and  magazines.  Moreover,  cellulosic  materials  are  currently  being  studied  as  green alternative energy sources as well as sources for platform chemicals.

## Inorganic polymers

Polymers are not only limited  to organic  materials  but  there  are  also  inorganic polymers  that  exist.  Inorganic  polymers  are  the  main  constituents  of  soil.  These inorganic  polymers  are  used  as  cutting  materials  (such  as  diamond,  silicon  carbide), coatings, flame retardants (e.g. asbestos), and building and construction materials (e.g. Portland cement).

The  name 'Portland' originated from the Isle of Portland, a peninsula in Great Britain. The stones quarried from the Isle of Portland is similar to the color of the cement. The word cement comes from the Latin word caementum ,  which literally means 'pieces of rough, uncut stone'. The dry cement is generally composed of anhydrous crystalline calcium silicates (tricalcium  silicate,  Ca3SiO5,  and  β-dicalcium  silicate,  Ca2SiO4),  lime  (CaO),  and alumina. The cement hardens when mixed with water due to the formation of crosslinks. The hardened cement is composed hydrated calcium silicates and calcium hydroxide.

## MATERIAL 10 - NANOTECHNOLOGY

## Nanomaterials

Nanomaterials are substances with at least one dimension between 1 and 100 nanometers. Nanomaterials possess unique size-dependent properties such as optical properties, and high surface area. Due to their properties, nanomaterials are being used and studied for various applications such as sensing, energy conversion and storage, catalysis, and drug delivery. In the field of nanomaterials, the most notable and highly researched  themes  are  related  to  carbon  nanomaterials,  metallic  nanomaterials,  and quantum dots.

## Carbon nanomaterials

Pure carbon materials exist mainly as either the sp 2 -hybridized graphite or sp 3 hybridized diamond. Carbon nanomaterials such as graphene, fullerenes, and carbon nanotubes possess unique physical and chemical properties such as chemical stability, good thermal and mechanical properties, and decent electrical conductivity. Due to their highly desired properties, carbon nanomaterials were able to find niche applications in various fields like environmental science, energy conversion and storage, and medicine.

Graphene is currently the thinnest existing material in the world. It is comprised of  sp 2 -hybridized  carbon  arranged  in  a  hexagonal  pattern.  Graphene  possesses noteworthy properties such as optical transparency, remarkable electronic conductivity, high thermal and mechanical properties, and large specific surface area. Graphene was accidentally  discovered  in  2004  by  eventual  Nobel  Prize  winners  Andre  Geim  and Kostya Novoselov. It was initially prepared by physically stripping a one carbon atom layer graphene from a graphite block using Scotch tape. There are several physical and chemical  methods  that  are  used  to  prepare  graphene  in  larger  quantities,  which includes sonication, pyrolysis, and chemical vapor deposition. Carbon nanotubes (CNT) is  a  cylindrical,  rolled-up  graphene  sheet.  Like  graphene,  the  carbon  atoms  are  sp2hybridized  and  are  hexagonally-arranged.  It  exhibits  similar  properties  as  graphene such as good thermal and electrical conductivity.

## Metallic Nanoparticles

Metal  nanoparticles  are  nanostructured metallic  substances  such as  silver  and gold  nanoparticles.  The  metallic  nanoparticles  may  come  in  various  shapes  such  as such as nanotriangles, nanocubes, and the very common nanospheres. The properties of  metallic nanoparticles are unique and drastically differ from their bulk counterparts. Usually, metallic nanoparticles dispersed well on various solvents such as water forming a  colloidal  metal  nanoparticle  dispersion.  Unlike  bulk  materials,  metallic  nanoparticles have relatively higher surface area making them ideal catalyst. Catalytic converters in car exhausts utilize precious metal nanoparticles (e.g. platinum nanoparticles) to control and to minimize the pollutants emitted by vehicles. Some metallic nanoparticles such as silver  nanoparticles  and  gold  nanoparticles  possess  distinct  optical  properties  in  the visible region due to the collective oscillation of surface electrons (or conductive band electrons)  upon  interaction  with  a  specific  wavelength  of  light.  This  phenomenon  is called localized surface plasmon resonance. Due to surface plasmon resonance, metal nanoparticles can be used for optical detection and diagnostic methods. For example, the  pregnancy  test  kit  utilizes  gold  nanoparticles  to  visually  detect  human  chorionic gonadotropin, a hormone found in urine during pregnancy.

## Quantum Dots

Quantum dots  are  nanoparticle  semiconductors  whose  sizes  range  between  2 and 10 nanometers (about 10-50 atoms). Cadmium sulfide, cadmium selenide, and lead sulfide are some notable quantum dots that are being studied and used today. Quantum dots exhibit unique electronic properties specifically size-controlled fluorescence emissions. By simply controlling the sizes of quantum dot crystals, specific colors can be produced. Large quantum dots with 7 nm size (150 atoms) in diameter produces red colors. The smallest quantum dots with 2 nm size (15 atoms) in diameters emits blue colors.  Due  to  their  intense  fluorescence  emission,  quantum  dots  were  studied  as potential drug delivery and bioimaging agent. Currently, quantum dots are commercially sold as TV displays and branded as QLEDs.
