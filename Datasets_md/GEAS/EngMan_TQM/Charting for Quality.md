# Variability

# Variability

Variability refers to the inherent fluctuations or differences that occur in processes. In a business context, variability can affect several factors including product quality, service delivery, production time, and customer satisfaction. In TQM, managing and reducing variability is critical because:

- Inconsistent Quality: Variability can lead to inconsistent quality in products and services, which can negatively affect customer satisfaction.
- Increased Costs: When processes are unpredictable, companies often experience higher costs due to rework, defects, or inefficient resource allocation.
- Lower Efficiency: Variability can cause delays, resource wastage, and failure to meet customer demands promptly.

<!-- image -->

<!-- image -->

# Variability

Variability in any system can be classified into two main types:

1. Common Cause Variation: This is inherent in every process and is considered normal. It arises from routine, systemic factors such as equipment wear, human error, or environmental influences. While it may not be immediately noticeable, over time it can lead to drift in process performance.
2. Special Cause Variation: This variation arises from specific, identifiable sources. These could include equipment breakdowns, changes in raw materials, or human errors that deviate from the standard process. Special cause variation typically indicates a problem that requires corrective action.

# Variability

Impact of Variability on TQM Outcomes 

- Improved Customer Satisfaction: Reduced variability leads to more consistent products and services, which enhances the customer experience and builds brand loyalty.
- Cost Reduction: By minimizing defects, rework, and wastage, organizations can lower operational costs. Consistent processes also reduce the need for costly inspections or interventions.
- Higher Efficiency and Productivity: Reducing variability allows for smoother operations, better resource utilization, and fewer disruptions, leading to increased productivity.
- Sustained Competitive Advantage: Organizations that successfully manage variability can maintain high levels of quality over time, which supports their competitive positioning in the market.

<!-- image -->

# Variability

Challenges in Managing Variability Despite the numerous benefits, there are challenges in controlling variability in the context of TQM:

1. Resistance to Change: Employees and managers may resist changes in processes, especially if those changes are seen as disrupting established practices.
2. Complexity of Identifying Root Causes: Some sources of variability are not immediately obvious, and finding the root cause of variability can be a lengthy and resource-intensive process.
3. Need for Data-Driven Decision Making: TQM relies heavily on data collection, analysis, and interpretation, which requires investment in technology and skilled personnel to manage and interpret data effectively.
4. Balancing Flexibility with Standardization: While standardization reduces variability, it can also make processes inflexible. In some cases, flexibility is necessary to adapt to changing customer needs or market conditions. Finding the right balance is key.

# Naïve Bayes Classifier

It is based on Bayes Theorem named after Thomas Bayes from 1700s

The Naive Bayes classifier works on the principle of conditional probability, as given by the Bayes theorem.

So, if we let  A and B be the two events associated with a random experiment. Then, the probability of A's occurrence under the condition that B has already occurred and P(B) ≠ 0 is called the Conditional Probability.
