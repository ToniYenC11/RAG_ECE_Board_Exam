## The Source

- ❖ The source of the information signal can be analog or digital
- ❖ Typical examples are video, audio, and digital data
- ❖ Sources may be described by the frequency range they occupy
-  Telephone-quality frequency range is 300 Hz - 3 kHz
-  Music frequency range is 20 Hz - 20 kHz
-  Video requires a frequency range from dc to 4.2 MHz

<!-- image -->

## Transmitter

- ❖ Is a collection of electronic components and circuits designed to convert the electrical signal to a signal suitable for transmission over a given communication medium.
- ❖ Transmitters are made up of oscillators, amplifiers, tuned circuits and filters, modulators, frequency mixers, frequency synthesizers, and other circuits.

<!-- image -->

## Communication Channel or Medium

- ❖ Is the medium by which the electronic signal is sent from one place to another.

## Gen. types of channel/ medium:

1. Wire- electrical conductors, coaxial cable, fiber optic cable, twisted pair
2. Wireless- free space
- Radio- any form of wireless communication
3. Other types of media- water (Sonar), Earth itself, AC power lines.

<!-- image -->

## Receiver

- ❖ Is a collection of electronic components and circuits that accepts the transmitted message from the channel and converts it back to a form understandable by humans.
- ❖ Receivers contain amplifiers, oscillators, mixers, tuned circuits and filters and a demodulator or detector that recovers the original intelligence signal from the modulated carrier.

<!-- image -->

## Attenuation

- ❖ Signal Attenuation , or degradation, is inevitable no matter what the medium of transmission.
- ❖ Attenuation is proportional to the square of the distance between the transmitter and receiver thereby reducing signal amplitude over long distances.
- ❖ Thus considerable signal amplification, in both the transmitter and receiver, is required for successful transmission.

<!-- image -->

<!-- image -->

## Noise

- ❖ Is an unwanted, random, undesirable electronic energy that enters a communication system via the communication channel/ medium and interferes with the transmitted message.

## Types of noise:

1. External noise- source of noise comes from outside of the receiver.
2. Internal noise- source of noise comes from within (components) the receiver.

<!-- image -->

## Noise and its Effects

- Noise is any unwanted signal (random) that corrupts and distort the desired signal
- Effects of Noise:
- Noise can cause the user to misunderstand the original signal
- Noise can cause the receiving system to malfunction
- Noise can result in a less efficient system

<!-- image -->

<!-- image -->

## External Noise

- ❖ Atmospheric noise- noise introduced by what is generally called 'STATIC'. Static is caused by lightning discharges in thunderstorms and other natural electric disturbances occurring in the atmosphere. (Lightning, Heavy rain, etc.)
- ❖ Extra terrestrial noise (SPACE)- noise introduced by heavenly bodies.
1. Solar noise- radiation of corona flares and
- sunspots. The solar cycle disturbances repeat themselves approximately every 11 years.
2. Cosmic/ Galactic noise- Two of the strongest sources, which were also two of the earliest Cassiopeia A and Cygnus A.

Summary: Space noise is observable at frequencies in the range from about 8 MHz to somewhat 1.43 GHz.

- ❖ Industrial noise/ Man made noise- spark producing mechanism (1 to 600 MHz), ignition sys., motors, switching machines, etc.

<!-- image -->

## Internal Noise

- ❖ ThermalAgitation, White or Johnson noise- noise generated in a resistance or the resistive components. It is due to random motion of the molecules (atoms and electrons) inside the component itself.
- ❖ Shot noise- it is caused by random variations in the arrival of electrons (or holes) at the output electrode of an amplifying device and appears as a randomly varying noise current superimposed on the output.
- ❖ Transit time noise- noise caused by the time it takes for a current carrier such as a hole or electron to move from the input to the output in transistors.
- ❖ Flicker- noise that occurs in resistors and conductors and is the result ofminute random variations of resistance in semiconductor material.

## Spectrum Management

Federal Communications Commission (FCC)- was formed in 1934 to regulate interstate and foreign communication.A primary function of the FCC is to allocate spectrum space, issue licenses, set standards, and police the airwaves. FCC handles both government and consumer issues. Its special focus is on six top concerns:

1. Homeland security- protecting telecommunication, broadcast, and other communication infrastructure.
2. Broadband- Encouraging the rapid availability to users of high speed switched, broadband telecommunication for voice, data, graphics and video.
3. Digital television- working with the industry to accelerate the transition to DTV.
4. FCC Reform- Attempting to be as efficient a bureau as possible. Recently, FCC added a media group and a division on wireline competition.
5. Media ownership- Promoting media ownership that fosters diversity, localism, and competition in the marketplace.
6. Spectrum policy- Supporting innovation and the efficient, flexible use of the spectrum, including issues such as interference protection, effective safety communication and international spectrum policies.
